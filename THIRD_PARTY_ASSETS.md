# Third-party assets

## Quaternius characters — CC0 1.0

The following free Standard editions were downloaded from the artist's official pages. Original license notices are retained in `assets/characters/*-LICENSE.txt`.

| Pack | Files used | Official source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Universal Base Characters | Female and male base mesh, long hair, simple parted hair, textures | https://quaternius.com/packs/universalbasecharacters.html |
| Modular Character Outfits — Fantasy | Female Ranger and Male Ranger outfits and textures | https://quaternius.com/packs/modularcharacteroutfitsfantasy.html |
| Universal Animation Library | Standard GLB animation library; seven clips are retargeted offline by `scripts/pack-characters.mjs` | https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html |

Local adaptations: mesh selection, head/neck and leg cropping, facial/waist adjustments, skin/hair/costume material changes, boots/shorts material regions, custom cloth face mask and cape, weapon attachment, animation retargeting and layered aiming. Textures were resized and encoded as WebP for delivery; duplicate textures were consolidated. The underlying character meshes and source animations are by Quaternius, not original project artwork.

The hashed delivery GLBs use lossless gzip compression. Baked animation tracks preserve every retargeted Float32 sample. Original source assets and an uncompressed compatibility path are retained.

The game does not contain extracted models, textures or audio from Ori, NieR, Chainsaw Man, Rozen Maiden, or other commercial reference works.

## Project-generated content

The terrain, enemy and weapon geometry, visual effects, custom cape/mask, procedural fallback score, and synthesized fallback sound effects are generated by this project's code. The recorded music below is third-party work, not original project composition.

## Recorded music — CC0 1.0

The source pages below identify these recordings as [Creative Commons CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). Author and license information was checked on 2026-10-08. The five adapted delivery files are stored locally in `assets/audio/`; the game does not stream them from OpenGameArt.

| Delivered file | Original work | Author | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `forest.ogg` | Iremos Forest Theme Loop | beardalaxy | [Original release](https://opengameart.org/content/iremos-forest-theme-loop) |
| `snow.mp3` | Fantasy Choir 1 | César da Rocha (cesisco) | [Fantasy Choir — 3 orchestral pieces](https://opengameart.org/content/fantasy-choir-3-orchestral-pieces) |
| `ash.mp3` | Fantasy Choir 2 | César da Rocha (cesisco) | [Fantasy Choir — 3 orchestral pieces](https://opengameart.org/content/fantasy-choir-3-orchestral-pieces) |
| `coast.mp3` | Fantasy Choir 3 | César da Rocha (cesisco) | [Fantasy Choir — 3 orchestral pieces](https://opengameart.org/content/fantasy-choir-3-orchestral-pieces) |
| `battle.mp3` | Determined Pursuit (epic orchestra loop) | Emma_MA | [Original release](https://opengameart.org/content/determined-pursuit-epic-orchestra-loop) |

Local adaptations: Iremos Forest Theme Loop and Determined Pursuit retain their complete source loops. Each Fantasy Choir piece has a 1.5-second end-to-start crossfade to prepare it for repeated playback. All five recordings were resampled to 32 kHz stereo, processed with a target of −21 LUFS integrated loudness and a −2 dBTP true-peak ceiling, and encoded to the listed Ogg or MP3 delivery format. These are processing targets, not a claim that lossy output measures exactly those values. The delivered recordings total 7,499,280 bytes (about 7.5 MB).

The game uses these recordings for forest, snow, ash, coast, and high-pressure/boss music, with fades between selections. The procedural score remains available for the sand biome and as a loading or failure fallback. Sources and adaptations are documented for provenance; this record does not claim a completed subjective listening review.

## Material sound effects — CC0 1.0

`assets/audio/material-sfx.mp3` is a locally hosted atlas of edited and layered CC0 foley/design assets. It replaces the default attack/contact sound for all 13 active weapons, major elemental spells, roll/blink/water movement, and selected creature material contacts. Original synthesized effects remain a loading/failure fallback. The new atlas is not audio extracted from Ori, Dead Cells, or other commercial games.

Each source page declares CC0 (the battle/bow pack offers CC0 among its license choices). This does not mean every source is a microphone recording: the bank combines field recordings, foley, and already-designed effects. The rifle/shotgun reports are designed from rubberduck’s recorded fireworks, not recordings of actual firearms.

Adaptations: onset editing, material layering, selected reverse textures for shadow magic, low/high-pass filtering, fades, per-role level matching, and mono 32 kHz MP3 encoding. Multiple takes use different source segments/layers; recorded playback does not randomly change pitch. `scripts/build-material-sfx.py` reproduces the atlas with Python standard library and FFmpeg from the downloaded source packages. [Machine-readable source and recipe record](docs/audio-material-sources.json) lists selected files, hashes, full source URLs, and exact transformations. Original source packs remain outside the shipping game.

The v94 edit shortens handheld weapon reports and recovery cues, separates pull/latch stages, removes contact layers from harpoon air swings, and uses breath/air textures with quieter wet accents for lanterns. Shadow magic keeps paper only for page handling. Per-cue playback gains range from 0.32 to 1. The current mono 32 kHz atlas uses 128 kbps MP3 encoding (910,124 bytes, 56.8 seconds, 130 unique edits and 70 lookup keys including aliases). These are new edits of the same licensed sources, not new claims of field recording or subjective listening approval.
